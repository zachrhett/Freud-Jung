About this archive

Why a complete Freud–Jung database cannot be a single free website—and what this one is instead.

Purpose

The Consulting Room is an educational front door to the Freud and Jung traditions: concepts, methods, public-domain books, case histories, study tools, and pointers to the institutions that hold the rest.

Why it is not “the complete works”

A genuinely comprehensive database of every letter, lecture, case file, and collected-works paragraph would require:

  • licenses from Freud’s literary heirs and from the Foundation of the Works of C. G. Jung;
  • licenses from Princeton, Routledge, Norton, and other publishers of translations and apparatus;
  • ethics review for unpublished clinical material;
  • a digital-humanities staff to transcribe Sütterlin manuscripts and align editions.

That is a university or foundation project, on the scale of the Library of Congress digitization or the Philemon Foundation, not a static brochure site. Anyone offering a one-click “complete Freud + complete Jung” download is usually mixing public-domain scans with protected translations.

Copyright, stated plainly

  • U.S. works published before 1931 are generally public domain.
  • Freud died in 1939. Many early books and early English translations are free in the U.S. Civilization and Its Discontents (1930) entered the U.S. public domain on 1 January 2026.
  • Jung died in 1961. In life-plus-70 jurisdictions his original texts remain protected until the end of 2031. Almost all standard English editions are separately copyrighted as translations and editorial work.
  • Letters and unpublished papers may have mixed ownership (Jung’s side, the correspondent’s side, privacy).

Original commentary on this site is written for education. Short quotations are used under fair-use / fair-dealing principles. Public-domain texts are linked at their official free hosts.

Not a clinic

Nothing on this site is psychotherapy, medical advice, or a diagnostic test. The workbooks are reading aids. If you need care, see a licensed clinician. In the United States, call or text 988 for the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.

How to extend this site

Natural next steps if you want to grow it into a real public resource:

  1. Ingest Gutenberg and Wikisource texts locally with their licenses intact, and add a search index (Pagefind or a small server).
  2. Build a bibliography database (Zotero → JSON) of every Freud and Jung title with publication date, translator, and rights flag.
  3. Apply for permission to quote longer passages from the Collected Works for a teaching anthology.
  4. Partner with a library to host IIIF viewers of PD scans.
  5. Keep clinical tools clearly labeled as historical / educational.

The files of this site are static HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. Open index.html in a browser or place the folder on any static host.
