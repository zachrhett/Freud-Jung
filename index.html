Why this site exists

A room for study, not a clinic

Sigmund Freud (1856–1939) and C. G. Jung (1875–1961) produced a body of work that still shapes how we talk about dreams, childhood, religion, sexuality, symbols, and the parts of the mind we do not own. This site gathers that tradition into a readable structure: biographies and concepts, clinical methods, famous cases, public-domain books, and links to the great archives.

It cannot host “everything they wrote.” Later translations, the Standard Edition, Jung’s Collected Works, most letters, and the Red Book remain under copyright. Where a text is free in the United States, we point you to it. Where it is not, we summarize, cite, and send you to the rights-holders and research libraries.

Clark University, 1909

The photograph that marks the crossing

In September 1909 Freud, Jung, and Sándor Ferenczi lectured at Clark University in Worcester, Massachusetts—the moment psychoanalysis was formally introduced to North America. Front row: Freud, G. Stanley Hall, Jung.

Within four years the friendship would collapse over the nature of libido, the role of sexuality, and the authority of the founder. The photograph remains the emblem of both alliance and the coming split.
