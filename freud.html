Founder of psychoanalysis

Sigmund Freud

Austrian neurologist. The talking cure, the unconscious as repressed wish, and a theory of mind that became a theory of culture.

Life in outline

1856 Born Sigismund Schlomo Freud in Freiberg, Moravia.
1885–86 Studies with Charcot in Paris; hypnosis and hysteria.
1895 Studies on Hysteria with Josef Breuer. The cathartic method.
1897–1900 Self-analysis after his father’s death. The Interpretation of Dreams.
1902 Wednesday Psychological Society, later the Vienna Psychoanalytic Society.
1909 Clark University lectures with Jung.
1914–23 Metapsychology papers; Beyond the Pleasure Principle; The Ego and the Id.
1938–39 Flight from Vienna to London after the Anschluss. Dies 23 September 1939.

The system

Freud’s psychoanalysis is three things at once: a method of treatment, a theory of the mind, and a method of reading culture. The central claim is that much of mental life is unconscious, that the unconscious is structured by conflict, and that symptoms, dreams, slips, and jokes are compromise formations.

Topographic model

Conscious, preconscious, and unconscious. The unconscious is not merely “not noticed”; it is actively kept out of awareness by repression. Dreams and free association are techniques for loosening that barrier.

Structural model (1923)

Id — the reservoir of drives, operating by the pleasure principle.
Ego — the organizing agency, reality-testing, defense.
Superego — internalized parental and cultural demands, source of guilt and ideal.

The ego is “not master in its own house.” Pathology is a matter of how the ego manages drive, prohibition, and external demand.

Drives

Early theory emphasizes sexuality (libido) in a broadened sense that includes infantile sexuality. After 1920 Freud pairs Eros (life, binding, sexuality) with a death drive (Thanatos): repetition, aggression, the return to an inorganic state. This dualism remains one of the most disputed parts of the theory.

Psychosexual development

Oral, anal, phallic, latency, genital. Fixation and regression explain adult character and symptom choice. The Oedipus complex is the nuclear conflict of the phallic period: love and rivalry toward the parents, resolved (in the classical account) by identification and the forming of the superego.

Defense

Repression is the prototype. Anna Freud and later ego psychology mapped further defenses: denial, projection, reaction formation, isolation, undoing, intellectualization, sublimation. A defense is not a moral failure; it is a strategy of the ego.
