Primary sources

Archives, museums, collected editions

The comprehensive record lives in libraries and rights-managed editions. This is the map.

Freud

Library of Congress — Sigmund Freud Papers

Tens of thousands of items digitized: correspondence (including with Jung), family papers, manuscripts, photographs, home movies, and Eissler interviews. The Library notes that a large portion is public domain in the U.S., but users must assess each item.

loc.gov/collections/sigmund-freud-papers

Freud Museum London

20 Maresfield Gardens: the study, the couch, the antiquities, Sigmund and Anna Freud’s libraries, and archives (including Ferenczi material). Research by appointment.

freud.org.uk

Sigmund Freud Museum Vienna

Berggasse 19, the long-time apartment and consulting rooms.

freud-museum.at

Standard Edition / rights

The Strachey Standard Edition remains the reference English text and is in copyright. Literary rights in Freud’s work have been administered through Sigmund Freud Copyrights / associated agents. New translations exist; check the publisher.

Jung

Foundation of the Works of C. G. Jung

Rights holder for Jung’s works. Literary agency: Paul & Peter Fritz AG, Zurich.

ETH Zurich — C. G. Jung Papers Collection

Manuscripts and correspondence. Access and copying of unpublished material generally require permission of the Foundation until rights expire.

C. G. Jung Institute / international societies

Training institutes in Zurich, London, New York, and elsewhere preserve lectures, seminar notes, and clinical tradition. These are professional bodies, not free text repositories.

Princeton University Press

Collected Works (Bollingen Series XX), letters, seminars, and the new Critical Edition beginning 2026. Purchase or library access. Digital CW is sold as a licensed edition.

press.princeton.edu

Research tools

  • PEP-Web (Psychoanalytic Electronic Publishing) — licensed database of journals and many books.
  • Philemon Foundation — unpublished and newly edited Jung seminars and the Red Book project.
  • Internet Archive — useful for pre-1931 scans; be skeptical of “complete works” bundles that include post-1930 translations.
