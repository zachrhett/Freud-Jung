Technique

Methods, evaluation, and diagnosis

How the work was actually done—and what “diagnosis” meant before the DSM.

These pages describe historical methods for study. They are not instructions for treating anyone, and they are not a substitute for licensed care.

Freud: the psychoanalytic situation

From hypnosis to free association

Breuer and Freud first used a cathartic method under hypnosis: recover the forgotten scene, discharge the strangulated affect, watch the symptom lift. Freud abandoned reliable hypnosis and replaced it with the fundamental rule: say whatever comes to mind, without selection or courtesy. The analyst listens with “evenly hovering attention,” resisting the urge to seize on the clever interpretation too soon.

Dream interpretation

The remembered dream is manifest content. Associations lead toward latent thoughts. Between them stands dream-work: condensation, displacement, considerations of representability, and secondary revision. Symbols exist, but Freud warned against treating a dream book as a code. The dreamer’s associations come first.

Resistance and transference

Resistance is anything that interrupts free association—silence, changing the subject, coming late, falling in love with the analyst as a way of not thinking. Transference is the transferring of old object-ties onto the person of the physician. In the mature technique, the analysis of transference is the treatment, not a side effect.

What Freud meant by diagnosis

He was not building a checklist manual. Diagnosis served two questions: Is this person treatable by analysis? What is the structure of the conflict? Rough classical groupings:

  • Hysteria — conversion and anxiety hysteria; repression of a sexual wish; symptoms as compromise.
  • Obsessional neurosis — isolation, undoing, reaction formation; ambivalence toward the loved object.
  • Phobia — displacement of anxiety onto an external object.
  • Narcissistic / psychotic positions — Freud was cautious; where transference could not form, he thought classical analysis reached a limit.

Later psychoanalytic diagnosis (PDM, Kernberg’s levels of personality organization, Nancy McWilliams) keeps this structural habit: how the person loves, defends, and tests reality, not only which symptom is loudest.

Jung: analytical method

Word association

At the Burghölzli, Jung and collaborators timed replies to a list of stimulus words. Delayed reaction, peculiar word, repetition, or bodily sign marked a complex. The experiment is one of the first empirical approaches to the affective unconscious. A study version is on the Tools page.

Dreams as compensation

A dream comments on the dreamer’s conscious attitude. If consciousness is too rational, the dream may be mythic or grotesque; if consciousness is inflated, the dream may cut the figure down. Jung reads series of dreams, not isolated nights.

Amplification

After personal associations are gathered, the image is widened through parallel motifs in myth, scripture, alchemy, and folk narrative. The aim is not to prove that the dreamer “is” a mythic hero, but to find the typical pattern in which a personal problem is caught.

Active imagination

A disciplined waking encounter with unconscious figures: write the dialogue, paint the scene, let the figure answer. The ego must remain present. Jung developed the method during his own confrontation with the unconscious; the Red Book is the private record of that work (published 2009; copyrighted).

Typology as a clinical tool

Knowing a patient’s type helps the analyst hear what is missing. An extraverted thinking type may need the inferior feeling or sensation function to become audible. Typology is a map of one-sidedness, not a brand.

Letters, lectures, training

Both men taught by letter as much as by book. Freud’s correspondence with Fliess, Jung, Abraham, Ferenczi, and Jones is a running laboratory. Jung’s letters (published in two volumes) often contain formulations that never entered the Collected Works in the same form. Lectures—Clark 1909, Freud’s Introductory Lectures, Jung’s Terry Lectures (1938)—were how the public first heard the method.

Training later institutionalized: training analysis, supervision, institutes. That apparatus is historical, not part of a website workbook.
