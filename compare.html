Alliance and rupture

Where they met, and where they parted

From 1907 to 1913 Freud and Jung were the public face of a new science of the mind. The break was personal, institutional, and theoretical.

Shared ground

  • The unconscious is real and causally powerful.
  • Symptoms have meaning; they are not only defective wiring.
  • Dreams, slips, and associations are data.
  • The relationship with the physician (transference) is part of the treatment, not an inconvenience.
  • Childhood and early emotional bonds shape adult life.

The break

Jung’s Wandlungen und Symbole der Libido (1911–12; English 1916 as Psychology of the Unconscious) redefined libido as psychic energy in general and read a patient’s fantasies through myth. Freud heard a desertion of the sexual etiology he considered the core of the movement. Letters grew colder. In 1913 Jung resigned as president of the IPA. Each later wrote the history of the other as a case.

How to read them together

They are not two flavors of the same therapy. Freud is a conflict psychologist of desire, authority, and civilization. Jung is a symbolic psychologist of meaning, development across the lifespan, and the religious function of the psyche. Later schools (ego psychology, object relations, self psychology, archetypal psychology, relational psychoanalysis) borrowed from both and argued with both.

For study: read one classic Freud case and one Jung essay on a symbol or a type in the same week. Notice what each theory cannot say easily. That gap is usually where the other writer is strongest.
